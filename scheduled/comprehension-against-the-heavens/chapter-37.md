Juno had invented the Four-Count Step because breaking a collarbone was educational.

That was how she described it, at least. Ye Xuan suspected the actual experience had been more painful and considerably less funny.

"One, two, three," she said from the edge of a low rooftop, "breathe. Four, release. You want the push after the body is ready to take it."

The early morning wind tugged at her red jacket. Below them, Manhattan was waking in layers: trucks, doors, distant sirens, a thousand people beginning a thousand separate routes.

Ye Xuan stood on the other side of the roof and copied her footwork.

One, two, three. Breathe. Four, release.

The qi in his dantian responded in a slow spiral. The stone in its copper holder stayed quiet against his ribs.

"You're thinking too much," Juno called.

"That's a familiar accusation."

"It's accurate."

He jumped from one painted line to another, no farther than a meter apart. His feet landed softly.

"Again," she said.

He went again. Faster.

Juno shook her head. "Your feet are fine. Your shoulders are late. You're taking the breath when the legs already need it."

He began the pattern again and found the delay she meant.

She was right.

That was irritating.

* * *

At the third layer of Qi Gathering, Ye Xuan could draw qi through his body without collapsing. It did not make him fast enough to match Juno in a straight line. Her Spring gift still owned the rooftops.

But his cultivation was becoming less like carrying water in a cracked cup. The qi remained in circulation after a movement, and sometimes the next motion felt easier instead of harder.

He had learned to use that small remainder.

Today, with the sun just touching the tops of the buildings, he felt something new.

His dantian was full.

Not in the sense of being unable to hold more energy; the space inside him had changed with each layer. It was full the way a lung becomes full before another breath requires an exhale.

He opened his sight on himself. The process was awkward, like trying to see the back of one's head without a mirror. Still, he could make out three fine loops around the gathering point, each stronger than the last.

Beyond them waited a fourth, incomplete circuit.

He had seen it for days.

Now, for the first time, he saw why it would not close.

He was holding his breath at the wrong moment.

* * *

"Stop," he said.

Juno landed from a jump and stared at him. "You've been practicing for twenty minutes."

"That's not what I mean."

He crouched and drew four arrows on the roof with chalk from his pocket.

"Your step uses a pause to store force," he explained. "But the pause belongs to your gift. It gives your coils time to load. I don't have your gift. I copied your timing without needing the same delay."

"And?"

"And every time I wait for the fourth beat, I interrupt the qi's circulation."

Juno folded her arms. "So don't wait."

"Exactly."

"Congratulations. You've discovered impatience. I've been telling you about it for weeks."

He grinned and drew three arrows instead of four.

The new rhythm did not eliminate breathing. It joined the breathing to the movement. Rather than gathering, pausing, and releasing, he let one motion complete the previous one: plant, turn, release. The breath continued through all three, unbroken.

He tried it without qi first.

His foot slipped on loose grit. He landed on one knee.

Juno laughed so hard she had to hold the parapet.

"Master of the universe," she gasped.

"I was testing the ground."

"You found it."

* * *

He tried again.

Plant. Turn. Release.

The movement took him across the roof in a clean diagonal. Not as far as Juno could jump, but without the little delay that had always made his fourth step feel artificial.

He repeated it six times. On the seventh, he let qi travel with the turn.

The incomplete circuit inside his dantian stirred.

Heat spread beneath his ribs. It was not the searing heat of injury, but a pressure that wanted to move. He had felt it before his first breakthrough and feared it then.

Now he understood.

The fourth loop was not another container. It was a return path.

He shifted his weight and breathed continuously through the step.

The loop closed.

For one terrible instant, every thread he could see brightened at once. The sunrise became a net of gold. The roof, the metal railing, Juno's Spring gift, even the water inside the building's pipes seemed connected by shining lines.

He heard the bell beneath the world.

The copper plate warmed but held.

Qi rolled through him in one complete circuit, reached his dantian, and began again.

Ye Xuan staggered.

Juno was beside him before he could fall.

"Hey."

"I'm fine."

"You look like a bad painting."

"I said I'm fine."

"That is not a medical opinion."

She eased him down against the wall and sat beside him until his breathing settled.

* * *

He looked inward cautiously.

Four loops.

The new one pulsed at the rhythm of his breathing, neither faster nor slower. Qi Gathering, layer four.

He could feel the difference in the smallest movements. Drawing his knees up no longer required the same deliberate attention. When he touched the wall, he sensed the texture of the mortar without opening full comprehension.

It was tempting to look at everything.

He resisted.

"Something happened," Juno said.

"Fourth layer."

She made a face. "You keep saying that like I should know what it means."

"Imagine a water tank with three pipes."

"Please don't."

"And then imagine adding a fourth pipe, but instead of letting the water run out, it brings the water back to the tank."

"I was happy five seconds ago."

He tried again. "I can keep moving longer before I get tired."

"That, I understand."

He rose carefully and demonstrated the Threefold Step once more. This time the diagonal covered nearly twice the distance. His landing was clean. The qi he used returned along the new loop instead of evaporating at once.

Juno watched in silence.

Then she said, "You stole my move."

"I improved a version of your move."

"Which is worse."

"I'd happily teach you."

"I already know how to move."

"And yet you insist on four."

Her eyes narrowed.

He knew that look.

He began to retreat.

* * *

Juno launched herself across the roof and struck with an open palm. Ye Xuan stepped aside in three beats. She adjusted in four and caught his sleeve.

"Too slow," she said.

He twisted out of her grip, planted, turned, and released. She missed his shoulder.

"Too smug," she corrected.

They traded harmless strikes across the painted lines, Juno faster and stronger, Ye Xuan finding new angles each time he avoided her. Twice he slipped. Once she caught him by the back of his shirt before he went over a low ledge.

At the end, he was panting. She was smiling.

"Okay," she said. "It's not bad."

"High praise."

"Don't make me take it back."

She offered her hand. He took it and let her pull him upright.

For a moment they stood close enough for him to see the copper coils of her Spring gift. They were still under strain, though less than when he first met her.

He almost reached toward one.

He stopped himself. A new layer did not give him permission to touch an injury he did not fully understand.

"Mateo?" he asked.

"Better. Carmen talked to his doctor. We're not signing the Respira paperwork until we know what's in it."

"Good."

Juno looked out across the waking city.

"You know," she said, "when I found you, you could barely climb a fire escape."

"When you found me, you were threatening to charge me for lessons."

"I still might."

"I can pay in pastries."

She considered. "Two pastries."

"One and a half."

"Don't start."

* * *

They descended the fire escape together. On the second landing, Ye Xuan paused.

Far below, a black car moved along the street and turned out of sight. There was no reason to think it was watching them. Half the cars in New York were black.

He tightened the strap of his bag and kept going.

Four loops of qi, a copper shield, three clean steps.

He was stronger than he had been yesterday. It was a fact he could measure.

It was not yet enough to protect everyone he was beginning to care about.

That was also a fact.

He intended to change the second one without forgetting the first.
