The Twins entered the Pit holding hands.

The crowd loved it. People shouted their names, pounded on the railing, and threw money toward the bookies before Mama Rocha's announcer finished explaining the rules.

Two against one. Special card. Seven hundred fifty dollars for the fighter who could last three rounds, a full win if both opponents surrendered or could not continue.

"You didn't say it would be two," Ye Xuan told Mama Rocha.

"You didn't ask."

"That sounds like something a contract lawyer would say."

She smiled thinly. "They're called Mirror and Shade. They've won every paired match this year. Want to walk away?"

Seven hundred fifty dollars.

He could almost hear his university invoice making a noise of approval.

"No," he said.

Juno caught his sleeve.

"You absolutely can walk away."

He looked at her. "If it gets bad, I will."

She searched his face for a lie. Unlike Mama Rocha, she couldn't taste one. Somehow that made the exchange feel more serious.

"Promise."

"I promise."

Only then did she let go.

* * *

Mirror and Shade were young men of the same height with the same dark hair and the same narrow, amused eyes. Mirror wore white wraps; Shade wore black. They separated at the bell and moved around Ye Xuan in opposite directions.

Their gifts were not strength or speed. They were two halves of one silver pattern.

A line ran from the back of each man's neck to the other's, thin as spider silk. Through it flowed a continuous exchange of sensory impressions: sight, balance, the pressure of feet on concrete, the position of hands, the direction of motion.

Ye Xuan saw an attack coming from Mirror's left and another forming in Shade's right shoulder.

He ducked the first.

The second caught him across the ribs.

The silver line brightened. Each twin had known where he would move because the other had already seen him move.

"They don't have blind spots!" someone shouted above.

That was not quite true.

They had shared blind spots.

* * *

He backed toward the wall and let them press him. He wanted information, not glory.

Mirror attacked high, Shade low. Ye Xuan jumped away from the sweep and took a glancing blow to the arm. The twins changed places without speaking. He opened comprehension wider, watching the silver line carry their perceptions back and forth.

Every system had a delay. Light did not cross a room instantaneously. Sound took time to travel. Nerves transmitted signals at finite speed. Even a supernatural link could be measured by when one man reacted to what the other saw.

Ye Xuan began counting.

Mirror saw his left foot shift.

One heartbeat later—no, far less than that—Shade adjusted.

A fraction of a second.

He needed to measure it more carefully.

He feinted right, then left. Mirror followed the first motion with his eyes. Shade moved to intercept it before the second was complete. The delay was tiny, but repeatable.

*There.*

They shared sight. They did not share one mind.

Two people still had to decide what the same signal meant.

* * *

He tried the Threefold Step.

Plant. Turn. Release.

He moved toward Mirror as if to strike, then redirected the momentum toward Shade at the exact moment the silver line carried the original intention across.

Shade blocked an attack that did not arrive.

Mirror lunged to cover him.

They collided shoulder to shoulder.

The crowd let out a delighted howl.

Mirror cursed at Shade. Shade shoved him away. For an instant, their expressions were not alike at all.

Ye Xuan stepped clear.

Mama Rocha was leaning forward behind the railing.

"You've got to be kidding," Juno muttered somewhere above him.

* * *

The Twins changed tactics. They stopped chasing him as a pair and began herding him toward the center, maintaining distance so each could cover the other's field of vision.

That was smarter.

He was not foolish enough to try the same trick twice without changing it.

He let himself appear tired. This required very little acting; the first exchange had left his ribs aching. He breathed through the new fourth loop and felt the qi return along its circuit. It bought him seconds, not invulnerability.

Mirror advanced. Shade circled.

Ye Xuan watched a loose paper cup skitter across the concrete beneath the rail. Somebody had dropped it. The thing was harmless, but it gave him an idea.

He kicked it lightly toward Mirror.

Both twins glanced down.

Shade's foot moved to avoid the cup a fraction after Mirror saw it. Mirror, who had already stepped over it, corrected his own movement in response to Shade's balance shift.

The shared pattern shivered.

They did not just receive one another's senses. They reacted to those reactions, building a loop.

Ye Xuan had seen a similar problem inside the first copper box he made for Seed Zero. A signal reflected into itself could grow louder with every pass.

He had solved it by spreading the signal across a broader path.

He could not solve it for the twins.

But he could make them notice the echo.

* * *

He moved in three beats to Mirror's left, then stopped hard. Mirror saw the stop. Shade felt Mirror's change of balance and stepped to compensate. Ye Xuan changed direction again, forcing the pair to process overlapping corrections.

Their movements lost their perfect symmetry.

Mirror swung where Ye Xuan had been.

Shade tried to cover him and found his brother already in the way.

Ye Xuan slipped between them.

For a second the silver link crossed directly through his line of sight. He could see the feedback clearly now: two images of the same movement arriving half a moment apart, each treated as new information.

The twins staggered.

It was enough.

Ye Xuan hooked Mirror's ankle, turning his rushing weight into a fall, and guided him to the floor. Shade lunged to help, but the linked sense of falling struck him before his own body moved. He threw his arms out instinctively and lost his balance.

Ye Xuan caught his sleeve and lowered him too, landing hard on one knee.

The move was not pretty. It was not especially strong. It was two ordinary people being made to confuse someone else's motion with their own.

He released them immediately.

"Stay still," he said. "Close your eyes. Let the link settle."

Mirror rolled away from Shade. Both were breathing hard.

Shade tried to rise. His hand went to the ground in the wrong place. He stared at it, startled, then at his brother.

"Enough," Mirror said hoarsely.

They exchanged a look.

Together, they raised their hands.

* * *

The Pit erupted.

Some people cheered. Others cursed the bookies. A man threw both his arms into the air and shouted that the entire evening was fixed, despite having watched the same fight as everyone else.

Ye Xuan climbed out with trembling legs.

Juno met him at the top and checked his ribs, his eyes, his hands.

"Three rounds," she said. "You promised you'd stop if it got bad."

"I didn't need three."

"You're impossible."

"I've been told."

She punched the uninjured part of his shoulder.

He took it as affection.

Mama Rocha sent for him before he had finished drinking water. In her office, she laid seven hundred fifty dollars on the desk and pushed it toward him.

"Three wins," she said.

"So I'm done?"

"For tonight." She opened the green leather ledger. "Two Saturdays from now. Iron Night. Special card. My champion Gravel. Winner gets five thousand."

Ye Xuan stopped counting.

Five thousand.

There it was again: the amount that could turn an impossible tuition deadline into a merely unpleasant one.

"What does Gravel do?" he asked.

Mama Rocha shut the ledger.

"He's very difficult to move."

"That sounds familiar."

"Not like Bricks." For the first time that evening, she didn't smile. "Bricks is heavy. Gravel makes the ground agree with him."

* * *

On their way out, Ye Xuan passed Mirror and Shade sitting on a bench. They were arguing quietly about which of them had misread the final movement.

"Both of you," he said.

They looked up.

"The link is good. You use it too much. When you disagree about what you see, the feedback multiplies."

Shade frowned. "How do you know?"

"Engineering."

Mirror stared at him. Then he laughed.

"Sure, kid. Engineering."

Ye Xuan left them arguing about whether that was an insult.

Outside, the city smelled of rain.

He had three victories, a job at the university, and the outline of a solution to his tuition problem. All he had to do was defeat a man who could make the ground agree with him.

He decided not to tell Juno what that sentence sounded like.

She already looked angry enough.
