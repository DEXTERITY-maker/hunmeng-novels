The second time Ye Xuan entered the Pit, the bookies knew his name.

That was not an improvement.

"Mott!" a voice shouted as he passed the railing. "Hey, Mott! The kid who made Bricks sit down!"

Another voice offered odds of six to one against him. Someone demanded a rematch with Bricks. Somebody else, who appeared to have lost money the previous week, threatened to throw Ye Xuan into the harbor if he won again.

Juno walked beside him with her arms folded.

"See? You're popular."

"I was happier when they laughed."

"That's because you don't understand how much trouble you are."

"I understand precisely that amount."

She gave him a look.

"All right," he said. "Perhaps a little less."

* * *

His opponent was called Hornet.

She was lean, sharp-faced, and restless, with short hair bleached almost white. She paced the opposite side of the pit while Mama Rocha's announcer told the crowd about her record: fourteen wins, three losses, six knockouts.

Her gift flickered at her shoulders, hips, and ankles: thin yellow threads that snapped into place before each step, forcing muscles to contract faster than their normal timing allowed. Around her right hand hovered a second pattern, narrower and crueler, gathering pressure into the joints of her index and middle fingers.

Two components. Speed and sting.

Ye Xuan studied the sting without staring. The pattern could deliver a sharp disruption through contact, scrambling signals in the nerves for seconds. Temporary paralysis, perhaps. Enough to put a fighter on the ground while Hornet finished the job.

He could not afford to find out by experiment.

"You the student?" Hornet called.

"Sometimes."

"Good. Tonight's lesson is short."

She bared her teeth.

The bell sounded.

She vanished.

Not literally. Ye Xuan understood that in the fraction of a second before her first strike arrived. Her gift accelerated movement but did not remove the need for contact with the floor. He could see the yellow threads tighten along her left calf, the hip follow, the shoulder turn.

Understanding was not the same as moving fast enough.

Her knuckles brushed his ribs.

A line of numbness spread across his side.

He stumbled out of reach just before her second strike snapped past his cheek.

The crowd roared.

* * *

He retreated in a circle, working his fingers against the numbness while he counted Hornet's steps.

One, two, three. One, two, three.

That was wrong. Juno moved in four. Hornet seemed to move in three, but there was a tiny pause before every third step, a correction when the nervous system caught up to the force of her gift.

He opened his sight wider.

Pain sparked behind his eyes.

Hornet attacked again. Ye Xuan dropped his weight and rolled away. Her fingers struck the wall at his back and left two tiny dark marks in the concrete.

If those fingers touched his neck, he might not get up.

He let the sight narrow.

A gift was a machine with a cost. Bricks had paid with his knees. Juno paid with tremors when she pushed too hard. Hornet paid by forcing her body to correct a rhythm it had not chosen.

He did not need to be faster.

He needed to arrive where she would be when the correction came.

* * *

Hornet feinted left, snapped right, and struck at his shoulder. He moved a quarter step before she committed. The sting missed his neck and brushed his sleeve.

He felt nothing in his right arm for one frightening instant.

Then he kicked off the floor, using a thread of qi to push his body sideways. It was a cheap use of strength compared with full comprehension, but the impact jarred his injured shoulder.

Hornet turned too quickly to follow.

Her heel scraped the concrete.

There was the correction.

Ye Xuan struck the floor beside her foot with his own heel. Not at her body. At the space she needed to step into.

She adjusted instinctively. The yellow threads in her legs crossed for a moment, dragging her weight in opposite directions.

She fell to one knee.

The crowd gasped.

Hornet sprang up with a curse and came at him harder.

Now she knew he could read her movement. That made her dangerous. She abandoned the repeating pattern and varied her speed in little bursts, using false starts and sudden stops. Twice she caught his forearm. Once she struck his hip hard enough to leave a bruise that burned with a dull numbness.

His dantian emptied by degrees. A headache grew behind his left eye.

He heard Juno shout his name from above.

He could not hear the rest.

* * *

For one moment, Ye Xuan considered using his power directly on Hornet's gift. He could find the fragile junction and snap it with qi. She might drop at once.

But he remembered Bricks asking what had happened to his knees.

A fighter could agree to being hit. She had not agreed to permanent damage that neither of them understood.

He chose the harder way.

Hornet drove forward, right hand extended, aiming for his throat.

Ye Xuan waited until she placed her lead foot.

Then he breathed with her.

One. Two.

The correction came before her third step. He didn't match her speed; he moved during the gap when her gift was pulling against itself. He pivoted inside the line of her sting, caught her wrist with both hands, and used the weight of her rushing body to turn her sideways.

A simple throw. Granny Liu would have scolded his footwork. Juno would have scolded the fact that he tried it at all.

Hornet hit the concrete shoulder first, rolled, and tried to rise.

Ye Xuan had already stepped back, leaving her room.

She sprang up, stumbled, and almost fell again. The yellow threads in her ankles flickered. Her own gift had forced one correction too many.

"Stay down," he said.

She glared at him. "Make me."

"I don't want to."

It was, apparently, the wrong thing to say.

She lunged with her right hand. Ye Xuan sidestepped, caught her forearm and guided her momentum into the wall. Her stinging fingers struck concrete instead of flesh.

A crack sounded.

Not bone. The brittle yellow pattern along her wrist had discharged into the wall. Hornet recoiled, shaking her hand. Her fingers were trembling.

Ye Xuan stood clear.

For three heartbeats neither moved.

Then Hornet raised her empty hands.

"Enough," she said.

The announcer's voice burst from the speaker.

"Winner: Mott!"

* * *

Mama Rocha paid him five hundred dollars with the expression of a woman who had expected trouble and received exactly the variety she had ordered.

"You're going to give me gray hair," she said.

"I thought you already had some."

"That's a very dangerous observation in my office."

She studied the red marks on his arm. "Hornet can leave a man unable to move for a minute. She barely touched you. How?"

"I watched her feet."

Mama Rocha tasted the answer. Her pale-gold threads went still.

"Well," she said, "that's irritatingly true."

Outside her office, Juno inspected his arm and shoulder. She was furious in the quiet way, which Ye Xuan had learned to fear more than shouting.

"You should have stopped after the first hit."

"Then I'd have lost."

"You might have kept your nerves."

"They're returning."

"That isn't an argument."

She took the envelope from his hand, counted it, and gave it back.

"Five hundred," she said. "Was it worth it?"

He thought of tuition, Mateo, the Respira letter, Ward's sealed case.

"No," he admitted. "But I needed it."

* * *

Across the Pit, the man in the green fleece stood behind the railing again. This time he was not pretending to watch the other fighters.

His phone was held steady, pointed at Ye Xuan.

Beside him stood a woman in a charcoal coat. She did not applaud. She watched the replay on the man's screen, then touched a finger to the image of Ye Xuan stepping into Hornet's broken rhythm.

"Not speed," she said.

Ye Xuan could not hear the words over the crowd. He saw the movement of her lips.

The man nodded.

The woman smiled, and Ye Xuan felt the copper plate in his pocket become warm.

He had hidden Seed Zero's pulse.

He had not hidden the fact that he could see what nobody else could.
