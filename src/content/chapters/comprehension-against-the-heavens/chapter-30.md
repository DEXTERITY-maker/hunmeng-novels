On Sunday afternoon, the floor of the Golden Lotus creaked, and Ye Xuan knew who it was before he looked up.

He was at the window table with Tommy, explaining percentages using a diagram of Zenith Prime's flight speed as a fraction of the speed of sound. Tommy was arguing that Zenith Prime would *definitely* break the sound barrier if he really tried, and that the diagram was therefore propaganda.

The floorboards by the door groaned—a deep, complaining sound, as if something much heavier than a man had stepped on them.

Special Agent Daniel Kroll stood in the doorway. No gray suit today. Jeans, a brown leather jacket that had seen better years, a Mets cap. He looked like any big middle-aged man on a Sunday, except for the way he held himself—very straight, very careful, the way people move when something hurts.

"Mr. Ye," he said.

Tommy looked from Kroll to Ye Xuan and back again. His eyes went very wide behind his taped glasses.

"Are you a *cop*?" he whispered.

"Bureau of Awakened Affairs," said Kroll.

Tommy made a sound like a kettle about to boil. "Are you *Awakened*?"

Kroll looked down at him for a moment. Then, very slightly, he shifted his weight from one foot to the other, and the floorboard under him let out a long, tortured creak.

"Maybe," he said.

Tommy nearly fell out of his chair.

* * *

Mrs. Tang sold Kroll a box of pineapple buns and a cup of tea, and watched him over her reading glasses the whole time with an expression that suggested she had not forgotten his last visit, or the gray suits, or what they had done to her customers.

Kroll took the tea and sat down carefully at the window table, across from Ye Xuan. The chair creaked under him. He lowered himself into it the way old men lower themselves into bathtubs—slowly, one hand on the table, his jaw tight.

"Homework?" he said, glancing at the diagram.

"Percentages."

"I was terrible at percentages." Kroll took a pineapple bun out of the box and looked at it. "My kid, too. She's fifteen now. Still terrible."

Tommy had been dispatched upstairs by his grandmother with threats. Ye Xuan could hear him on the stairs, not going up, listening.

"Is this an official visit?" Ye Xuan asked.

"It's Sunday." Kroll bit into the bun. Chewed. Swallowed. "I don't work Sundays. I'm just a guy buying pastries."

"From Chinatown. Where you don't live."

"My wife likes the pineapple buns." Kroll looked at him steadily. "And I wanted to see how you were doing. You were pretty sick last time."

"I'm better."

"I can see that."

* * *

He could see it. Ye Xuan knew he could. A month of tai chi at dawn and qi in the evenings and three layers of Qi Gathering had changed him, and the changes were not subtle. The hollows in his cheeks had filled in. The shadows under his eyes were gone. His skin was clear, almost glowing. He had put on muscle—not much, but enough that his T-shirt fit differently across the shoulders. He stood straighter. He moved differently.

He looked, in short, nothing like the starving, feverish boy who had sat at this table a month ago and told Kroll he couldn't remember anything.

Kroll's eyes moved over him slowly. Face. Hands. Shoulders. The way he sat.

"You look like a different person," Kroll said.

*You have no idea,* Ye Xuan thought.

"I got a job," he said aloud. "Courier. Running all day. It's good exercise."

"Skyline Couriers. Lou Castellano." Kroll nodded. "I know Lou. Good guy. Lot of Awakened working for him." He paused. "Rocco Vitale is in Bellevue Hospital with a cracked shoulder blade and a dislocated wrist. He says he fell down some stairs."

"That's terrible."

"Isn't it." Kroll took another bite of his bun. "Strange thing. Rocco's been arrested eleven times. Never once told a cop anything. Never once said a word about anybody. But a guy I know at the precinct says that when they asked Rocco who hurt him, he got this look on his face. Scared. He's never looked scared before. And he said—" Kroll paused, as if remembering the exact words. "*Ask the bakery boy.*"

The bakery was very quiet. Behind the counter, Mrs. Tang had stopped wiping the glass.

"Was it you?" Kroll asked.

Ye Xuan thought about lying. He thought about Shah's voice: *Everybody lies to the Bureau.*

"No," he said. "Rocco and three of his friends tried to strangle me in an alley. A friend of mine kicked him off me. He landed on some dumpsters."

Kroll looked at him for a long moment.

"A friend with a gift," he said.

"A friend who's very good at kicking."

The corner of Kroll's mouth moved. It wasn't quite a smile, but it was close.

"Rocco's been shaking down this street for a year," he said. "Nobody would testify. Nobody would press charges. Now he's in Bellevue, and his crew hasn't been seen on Mott Street in a week." He finished the bun. "I'm not going to ask any more questions about that."

* * *

He shifted in his chair.

It was a small movement, just a change of position. But Ye Xuan saw Kroll's jaw tighten, and the knuckles of the big hand on the table go white for a second, and he heard the faint hiss of breath through Kroll's teeth.

And without meaning to—without wanting to—he looked.

The chains were still there, wrapped around Kroll's bones: dark gold, heavy, dense, every link in its place. A month ago, they had looked perfect to him—the most orderly gift he had ever seen, like a machine built by a master craftsman.

He had been at the first layer of Qi Gathering then. He had seen the shape.

At the third layer, he saw more.

The chains were too heavy.

Not for the gift. For the man. Every link pulled down, all the time, even when Kroll wasn't using his weight. And all that weight, all day, every day, for twelve years, rested on one place: his spine. Ye Xuan could see it. The chains wrapped thickest around the vertebrae of Kroll's lower back, and there, between the third and fourth and fifth vertebrae, the bones were compressed. Squeezed together. The soft discs between them flattened almost to nothing. And on the chains themselves, right at that spot, there were cracks—thin, dark lines running across the gold links, the way cracks run across old porcelain.

*He's crushing himself,* Ye Xuan thought. *His own gift is crushing his spine.*

He made himself look away.

"Mr. Ye?"

"Sorry." Ye Xuan blinked. "I was thinking."

Kroll studied him. Then he glanced down at his own hand, at the white knuckles, and slowly relaxed it.

"Old back injury," he said. "Marines. It acts up when it rains."

It wasn't raining. It hadn't rained in a week.

Ye Xuan said nothing.

* * *

Kroll stood up to go. He did it slowly, the way he had sat down, one hand on the table. The floor groaned.

At the door, he stopped.

"One more thing," he said. "Agent Shah."

Ye Xuan waited.

"She got a formal reprimand on Thursday. For accessing closed case files without authorization." Kroll's voice was very even. "Silas Crane's file, among others. And some old documents from a monitoring program that she wasn't cleared to see anymore."

"Is she okay?"

"She's suspended for two weeks. Without pay." Kroll looked at him. "She's a good analyst. Best I've ever worked with. But she's young, and she thinks if she finds the truth, the truth will protect her." He paused. "It won't. Not in this city. Not from the people who closed that file."

He opened the door. The bell jingled.

"Whatever she's doing," Kroll said quietly, "she's doing it alone now. The Bureau's not behind her. *I'm* not behind her—I can't be, not officially." He looked at Ye Xuan very directly. "So if you're doing it with her, Mr. Ye, then you be careful. And don't get her killed."

He went out.

Through the window, Ye Xuan watched him walk down Mott Street, big and slow and very straight, holding himself the way a man holds a stack of plates he's afraid to drop.

* * *

Tommy came thundering down the stairs the moment the door closed.

"He's *Awakened!*" he shouted. "A *Bureau agent* who's *Awakened!* Did you see the floor? Did you *see* it? What's his gift? Is it weight? It's weight, right? Like a gravity thing? That's so cool—"

"Tommy," said Mrs. Tang. "Upstairs. *Now.*"

Tommy went, still shouting.

Mrs. Tang came out from behind the counter, picked up Kroll's empty teacup, and stood holding it, looking at the door.

"That man is in a lot of pain," she said.

Ye Xuan looked at her in surprise.

"My husband walked like that," she said, quietly, almost to herself. "At the end. The last year. Like every step was carrying something." She turned the cup in her hands. "He said it didn't hurt. He always said it didn't hurt."

She went back behind the counter.

Ye Xuan sat at the window table and looked at the diagram of Zenith Prime's flight speed and didn't see it.

He was thinking about cracks in golden chains.

He was thinking that he had untangled a strand of Juno's coils with one thread of qi. That Tank's back had stopped hurting after a week of breathing.

He was thinking that Kroll's chains were the most complicated gift he had ever seen, and that if he tried to touch them and got it wrong, he might do more than fail. He might break them.

*Not yet,* he told himself. *You're not ready. Not yet.*

But he took out his notebook and, under the list of rules, he wrote a single word in small letters:

*Kroll.*
