They chose the alley well.

It ran between Elizabeth Street and the Bowery, a narrow canyon of brick with fire escapes climbing both walls and a row of dumpsters along one side. Ye Xuan used it every evening as a shortcut from the last delivery back to Canal Street. Wren had told him about it on his second day. Apparently Rocco had found out about it too.

It was twenty past six. The sky above the rooftops was orange, but the alley was already in shadow.

Rocco stood at the far end, blocking the way to the Bowery. He was wearing a black brace on his right elbow—a thick sleeve of neoprene and steel, the kind athletes wore after surgery.

*He learned,* Ye Xuan thought, and felt a small, cold respect.

Behind him, a car door slammed. He turned. The skinny one with the toothpick—Joey—had stepped into the alley from Elizabeth Street. Next to him, the round one, Benny. And a woman Ye Xuan had never seen before: short, thin, with bleached hair and enormous mirrored sunglasses, although the sun had already gone down behind the buildings.

Four of them. Front and back.

"Bakery boy," Rocco said, almost warmly. "You took a shortcut."

* * *

Ye Xuan didn't answer. He was already looking.

His sight opened, smoothly now, without a flinch—the second layer of Qi Gathering had made it easier, cheaper, like a muscle that had grown. He let it skim across them, one at a time.

*Rocco.* The iron-gray cables in his forearms, wound tight. The knot at the elbow—now hidden under steel and neoprene, the pattern pressing against the brace from inside. He couldn't reach it through that. Not easily.

*Joey.* Thin threads in his arms, too, but different: long and loose and pale, like rubber bands coiled in a drawer. They ran from his shoulders to his fingertips, and they were *stretchy*. Ye Xuan understood it at once. Joey's arms could extend. A meter, maybe more. But the bands were slow to pull back—after each stretch, they'd take a second to contract.

*Benny.* A dense, heavy knot of brown threads in each fist and nowhere else. His punches would land like a sledgehammer. But his legs were ordinary—no pattern there at all. Slow feet. Heavy hands.

*The woman.* Her gift was in her eyes. Behind the mirrored glasses, two bright white points of light, tightly wound, like the filaments in a camera flash. Charging.

*Flash,* he thought. *She blinds people.*

Four gifts. Four patterns. Four sets of strengths and weaknesses laid out in front of him like a diagram in a textbook.

It was a strange, cold, beautiful feeling, seeing it all.

It didn't change the fact that he was one skinny engineering student with a half-full dantian, in an alley, against four Awakened who wanted to break him.

* * *

"Dee," said Rocco.

The woman took off her sunglasses.

Ye Xuan closed his eyes.

He did it a fraction of a second before the light came—he'd seen the filaments brighten, the pattern tightening to release—and even through his closed eyelids the alley went white, a blinding, burning white like staring into a camera flash at point-blank range. Someone yelped. Joey, maybe, who'd been looking the wrong way.

Ye Xuan kept his eyes closed and moved.

He couldn't see. But he could *feel*. There was qi in the alley—thin, the way it was everywhere indoors and in narrow places—and the four Awakened stood in it like rocks in a stream, their gift-patterns stirring the threads around them. It wasn't sight. It was more like knowing where the furniture was in a dark room you'd lived in all your life.

Benny was coming at him from the left. Heavy, slow, the brown knots in his fists blazing.

Ye Xuan stepped right, and Benny's fist went past his ear like a passing truck and hit the brick wall. Bricks cracked. Benny howled.

Ye Xuan opened his eyes.

The flash was fading—Dee's filaments had burned out, recharging. He had maybe ten seconds before she could do it again. Joey was blinking, half-blinded, his long arms swinging. Benny was clutching his hand, staring at the cracked wall.

Rocco was coming down the alley, not running, walking, the way a truck walks.

*Ten seconds.*

Ye Xuan went for Dee.

He covered the distance in three steps, and she flinched back, her filaments flickering—not ready, not charged—and he did the only thing he could think of. He grabbed the mirrored sunglasses out of her hand and jammed them back onto her face, crooked, upside down.

"Keep them on," he said. "It's for your own good."

Dee stared at him through upside-down lenses, too surprised to move.

Something whipped toward his head.

He ducked, and Joey's fist went over him—a fist on the end of an arm that was suddenly two meters long, swinging like a rubber hose. It smashed into a dumpster lid with a bang. Ye Xuan saw the pale bands in the arm stretched to their limit, thin, trembling, and he saw them begin—slowly—to contract.

*One second to pull back.*

He grabbed the stretched arm by the wrist with both hands and pulled.

Joey yelped and stumbled forward, dragged by his own extended arm, and Ye Xuan let go just as the bands snapped back. Joey's arm contracted like a released slingshot, and Joey went with it—straight into Benny, who was turning around with his sledgehammer fist raised.

The fist hit Joey in the shoulder. Both of them went down in a heap.

* * *

Then Rocco reached him.

There was no time to dodge. The big man's left hand—his *left*, the one without the brace—closed on the front of Ye Xuan's jacket and slammed him back against the brick wall. Breath exploded out of his lungs. His head hit brick. Stars burst in his vision.

"You know what I did all week?" Rocco said conversationally. His face was very close. "I watched videos. Martial arts stuff. Joint locks. So I could understand what you did to me." His right arm came up, the one with the brace, and the hand settled around Ye Xuan's throat. Not squeezing yet. Just resting there. "You know what I figured out? There's nothing on the internet that does what you did. Nothing. So what are you?"

The fingers began to tighten.

Ye Xuan couldn't breathe. He grabbed Rocco's wrist with both hands, and it was like grabbing a steel bar. The iron-gray cables in the forearm were blazing, squeezing, and the knot at the elbow was under the brace, out of reach—

*The brace protects the elbow,* he thought, his vision going dark at the edges. *The knot is at the elbow. But the cables—the cables don't stop at the elbow. They run all the way down. All the way to—*

He saw it. Right there, under his hands.

At the wrist, where the forearm met the hand, the cables narrowed again. Not as much as at the elbow—not a true knot—but they bunched together, all of them, passing through the narrow channel of the wrist bones like threads through the eye of a needle.

He had two threads of qi left in his dantian. He pushed both of them down his arms, into his thumbs, and drove his thumbs into the inside of Rocco's wrist.

The cables stuttered.

Not completely, not the way they had at the elbow. But enough. The fingers around his throat loosened for one second—

—and from somewhere above them, a voice said, "Hey, Rocco."

* * *

Juno came down off the fire escape like a red comet.

She didn't jump. She *fell*, three floors, feet first, and at the last moment, one, two, three, four, her coils released and she kicked off the air itself, and both her sneakers hit Rocco Vitale square between the shoulder blades.

He flew.

He literally flew—a hundred kilos of Awakened racketeer lifting off his feet, crossing the alley, and crashing into the row of dumpsters with a noise like a car accident. The dumpsters toppled. Garbage exploded across the alley.

Juno landed in a crouch beside Ye Xuan, light as a cat.

"You're welcome," she said.

Ye Xuan slid down the wall, gasping, both hands at his throat.

At the far end of the alley, Joey and Benny were untangling themselves. Dee was trying to get her sunglasses the right way up. Rocco was not getting up; he was lying in a pile of garbage bags, groaning, and when he tried to lift himself on his right arm, the arm wouldn't hold him.

Joey looked at Rocco. Then at Juno, who was standing with her hands in her jacket pockets, rocking slightly on her heels, as if she would really, really like an excuse to do that again.

"Go home, Joey," Juno said. "Take your boss. He needs a doctor."

They went. It took all three of them to lift Rocco, and they half-carried, half-dragged him out of the alley toward Elizabeth Street. At the corner, Rocco looked back once. His eyes found Ye Xuan's.

Then they were gone.

* * *

The alley was quiet except for the drip of something leaking from an overturned dumpster.

Juno crouched in front of him. She looked at his throat, where Rocco's fingers had left red marks. At his nose, which was bleeding freely now, down over his lip and chin. At the scraped skin on the back of his head where it had hit the brick.

"You look terrible," she said.

"I was winning."

"You were being strangled."

"Strategically."

Juno made a sound that might have been a laugh. Then her face got serious.

"Wren called me," she said. "She heard the car doors on Elizabeth from the garage. She said, four of them, and they're going into Ye's alley." She paused. "I was on a run to Tribeca. I came as fast as I could."

"You came over the rooftops from Tribeca?"

"Over the rooftops is faster." She stood up and held out her hand. "Come on. Garage. I'm not carrying you, so you'll have to walk."

He took her hand. She pulled him up easily—she was much stronger than she looked—and for a second, standing very close in the dark alley, he saw her clearly with his ordinary eyes: the scrape on her cheekbone from some earlier run, the braid coming loose, the dark eyes watching him with an expression he couldn't read.

And then, because his sight was still half-open, he saw something else.

In her legs, the bright copper coils of her gift were trembling.

Not from effort. Not from the jump. They were trembling the way an old wire trembles when too much current runs through it—and along their edges, here and there, the bright copper had gone dark, frayed, like the end of a rope that has been cut and left to unravel.
