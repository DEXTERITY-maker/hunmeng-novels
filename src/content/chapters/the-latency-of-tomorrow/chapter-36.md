The technical finding was four pages long. Yanchuan read the title before he looked for his brother's name.

Independent review of acceptance records, October 29 test.

The document's conclusion was careful and difficult to misunderstand. The available evidence did not substantiate the final package's unqualified claim that the full agreed load had met every acceptance condition. The selected comparison excluded an auxiliary cooling demand and an unsettled interval that materially affected the meaning of the result. The reviewers could not locate the required restoration acknowledgment among the records supplied.

The finding did not say the equipment would always fail. It did not say anyone had intentionally falsified a document. It did not identify why Yansheng had disappeared.

Amina read it with Yanchuan during a scheduled call.

“They're asking for a corrected acceptance process,” she said. “The operator can set the next test conditions. They have to decide what corrective work is needed.”

“What happens to the final signature?”

“The document controller is reviewing its authorization trail. That's a separate finding.”

“Will someone lose their job?”

“I don't know. That isn't in my gift to predict.”

He looked at the unsigned note that had carried his brother's original question. Can these two summaries both be right?

The answer had become more precise over months. The tidy summary could truthfully describe operation under a narrowed condition, but it had been presented as evidence for a broader promise it did not establish. Those things could not be made equivalent by agreeing at the bottom of a spreadsheet.

Yanchuan wrote an explanation for An that did not depend on terms she had not used.

“The equipment may work under some conditions,” he said. “The signed report made a claim about more conditions than the evidence demonstrated.”

She listened and asked, “Does this change the missing-person inquiry?”

“It gives a documented reason he may have wanted the original maintenance record.”

“May have.”

“Yes.”

She took the answer without urging him to go further.

That afternoon the employer issued a statement to those who had received its earlier acceptance summary. It acknowledged the review and said the operator would determine whether another demonstration was required. Luo sent a separate note to the family, expressing regret for the way the company's early movement summaries had been framed.

Yanchuan wanted to ask whether regret came with a name attached.

An asked for the list of recipients to whom the corrected movement statement had been distributed. It mattered more to her than the expression of regret. They compared the list with the contacts already involved in the missing-person case and asked that any omissions be corrected.

Guo called Chen on the same day. His firm was considering a broader portfolio of projects with similar contractual performance promises. The independent finding from Kenya was not publicly available for him to use, and Yanchuan had shared none of its restricted material. Still, the generic problem in his own feasibility work had become more urgent: which measurements were required to price real availability?

Guo asked whether a demand-management pilot could answer that question for one local factory.

“For that factory, within a defined period,” Yanchuan said.

“Not for every factory.”

“Not without measuring them.”

Guo laughed. “You have a way of making a small proposal sound smaller.”

“It's how I keep it true.”

They agreed to prepare a bounded pilot with a visible human stop. The client would own its operational records, and the engineering team would have permission to report unfavorable findings to the client, not hide them in a commercial presentation.

The commercial terms still required work. Yanchuan did not confuse an agreement in principle with a signed contract.

At night his father called. He had read the family explanation and wanted to know whether it meant Yansheng had been right.

“He was right to ask the question,” Yanchuan said. “The final report claimed more than the evidence supported.”

“And that was why he went missing?”

“We don't know.”

Jianming breathed slowly into the phone. He had reached an age when pauses sometimes concealed pain, and Yanchuan asked after his wrist before returning to the case.

“It's improving,” his father said. “Qiao made me keep the appointment.”

“Good.”

“Don't say good like you're her supervisor.”

Yanchuan smiled.

Afterward he sent An the final technical finding with its limitations preserved. They added it to a new section of the chronology: results that changed what could be said publicly. The missing-person section remained open.

For the first time, Yanchuan had a conclusion he would have been satisfied to defend at work even if his brother had never sent the file.

That should have made him proud. Instead he felt the absence beside it.

He had proved that a report made an unsupported claim. He had not proved that the report's defect had endangered Yansheng, nor that anyone knew where he was. A technical conclusion could be both important and painfully insufficient.

He closed the four-page document and opened a photograph of his brother repairing the toy excavator with the missing wheel. The paper flag reading awaiting part had amused everyone when it was taken.

A finding was not an answer. It was the part of an answer they had earned.

The next morning the corrected acceptance claim moved through the operator's document system. The case reference remained unchanged. Yanchuan went to work, where another client had asked him for a single figure and an assurance that it would hold in every summer.

He typed the operating temperature range, selected the tested period, and left the guarantee field blank. Then he called the client to ask which summer they meant.
