Han Rui had written DO NOT BORROW on his clamp meter in ink that had survived four changes of employer. He handed it to Yanchuan and said, “Don’t touch anything.”

They stood behind the safety line at a test workshop outside Shenzhen. Han supervised maintenance for a company that serviced industrial power equipment. He had known Yanchuan since a graduate placement, when the younger man had tried to diagnose a faulty pump from a dashboard whose sensor was disconnected.

The memory had remained useful to Han long after it stopped being funny to Yanchuan.

“Mei says you need a comparison,” Han said.

“Similar equipment. Not the same model.”

“Good. Keep saying that.”

The workshop manager had agreed to share a limited record from a scheduled demonstration. No customer information, no sealed cabinets opened for their benefit, no changes to the test plan. Yanchuan signed the visitor sheet and listened to the briefing. His brother had spent years making sure other people did these dull things.

A technician named Peng attached the independent instrument while the system was isolated as required. When the demonstration began, they watched from the marked area. Fans rose in pitch. A green lamp changed to amber, and the screen paused before continuing with a new row.

“That pause,” Yanchuan said.

“Which pause?” Han asked.

The question irritated him until he checked. The display had paused; the record had not. Somewhere below the picture, events were still being written. He made a note before speaking again.

Afterward, Peng supplied the permitted export and the separate instrument record. Han took them to a meeting room where the tea had been brewed until it tasted of wood.

Yanchuan expected the two records to disagree. They did, a little. Enough to make his pulse quicken before Han pointed at the units and the averaging interval.

“You’re comparing a window with a moment.”

They put both records on the same basis. The discrepancy became smaller. When they excluded the demonstration’s unsettled period, the chart became remarkably smooth.

“And there’s your miracle,” Han said.

“It’s reporting.”

“Sometimes reporting is where the miracle happens.”

Yanchuan could reproduce the appearance of reassurance without a broken instrument and without inventing a single sample. That did not clear the supplier in Kenya. It changed the question. An accurate meter could still feed a misleading claim.

He thought of the clean cooling chart his client had wanted. He had not falsified it. He had merely allowed its caption to become more confident than the work beneath it.

Han watched him close the spreadsheet.

“What?”

“Something at work.”

“Then deal with that too. Don’t become honest only about your brother.”

Yanchuan called Chen from the yard. He explained the missing qualification in the client draft and asked that it be restored. Chen was silent for several seconds.

“It’s still a recommendation?”

“Within the tested range.”

“Then say that in a sentence people can understand.”

When Yanchuan returned, Han had found an old photograph of Yansheng on his phone. They had met at a commissioning job three years earlier. Yansheng was crouching beside an open packing crate, holding a part that had arrived with the wrong label.

“He made them check every crate,” Han said. “The manager was furious. Two more were wrong.”

“You never told me you worked together.”

“You never asked about his work. You said he travelled a lot.”

The remark landed without emphasis. That made it harder to escape. Yanchuan knew his brother’s jokes, his old injuries, his preference for the corner seat. He knew almost nothing about the hours that paid for those train tickets home.

With the manager’s permission, he sent An the comparison record and a short account of what the test could establish. Different equipment, similar reporting effect. It could explain how the summaries diverged. It could not explain a disappearance.

Han walked him to the gate. A truck was waiting for an inspection slot. Its driver had spread a newspaper across the steering wheel and was eating an orange.

“If you go there,” Han said, “find people who know the place. You know a sheet of numbers.”

“I know.”

“You know it here.” He touched the folder under Yanchuan’s arm. “Remember it when you’re frightened.”

On the train back, Yanchuan looked again at the photograph of the packing crate. For the first time that week, his brother appeared to be doing something other than disappearing.
