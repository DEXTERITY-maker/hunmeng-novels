The hearing took place at ten in the morning, in a room with open curtains and a broken water dispenser.

It was not a criminal trial. The operator had arranged a formal review of the acceptance process after its technical finding, with representatives of the contractors and people responsible for maintaining the installation. Amina attended as an engineer whose work had intersected with the records. Wanjiru was available to advise the family about what could be shared, but the missing-person inquiry remained separate.

Yanchuan participated remotely. He had asked twice whether his presence would help. The answer was that he could listen to the technical portion and submit documented questions through the appropriate channel. He could not question workers as if the meeting belonged to him.

At the beginning the chair read the scope. The purpose was to establish what conditions had been tested, which deficiencies remained open, how the final acceptance package had been assembled and what correction was required. The review would not decide who caused Yansheng to disappear.

The words were familiar. They still hurt.

The document controller, Kamau, explained how an attendance acknowledgment had appeared as an approval mark in the later package. The system permitted approved signature images to be attached to a document set, but the available record did not show authorization for using Yansheng's acknowledgment to certify closure of the outstanding item.

“Does that prove someone deliberately reused it?” a contractor representative asked.

“No,” Kamau said. “It identifies an assembly whose approval trail is incomplete. We can trace the document's versions. Intent requires a separate assessment.”

Amina described the temporary cooling arrangement and the later demonstration that had stopped at the agreed temperature condition. She named the measurement boundaries carefully. When a manager tried to combine the stopped run with a claim about efficiency under lower demand, she asked him to keep the two tests distinct.

Daniel's original maintenance note was discussed without asking him to appear. His family had agreed to the limited use of the relevant technical record after consulting the people advising them. His injury, which occurred before the acceptance demonstration in a separate handling accident, was not presented as evidence of a failure during that test.

Yanchuan was grateful. He knew how easily someone else's suffering could have been folded into a more exciting account of his brother's disappearance.

The chair asked whether the full acceptance claim had been supported.

The reviewers said no. The available records described operation under narrowed conditions, with a missing restoration acknowledgment and a change in auxiliary demand that had not been made clear in the unqualified summary.

The chair asked what would be necessary for a new approval. They listed the agreed load boundary, restoration verification, properly recorded stop conditions, accessible event logs and signatures tied to the decisions they actually authorized.

No one declared the entire installation useless. No one declared it safe for every promised operating state.

During the break Yanchuan called An.

“It's being said aloud,” he told her.

“What is?”

“That his signature didn't approve what they said it approved.”

“Good. Make sure the written finding says it too.”

He heard dishes in the background. Their son was preparing for a school excursion and could not find his raincoat.

After the break a contractor manager expressed regret for confusion and asked to move directly to corrective testing. Kamau insisted that the revision history be completed first. Amina supported him. An unexamined document process could reproduce the same mistake even after a successful technical repair.

The hearing ended with assigned actions and dates. The operator would publish a corrected acceptance status, preserve relevant records and schedule further testing when the required conditions had been met. Workers' safety representatives would be consulted before changes to operational limits.

Yanchuan expected satisfaction. Instead he felt tired enough to sleep at his desk.

Wanjiru called when the session ended.

“Was it useful?” he asked her.

“For the technical question, yes. It records what is established and who must do the next work.”

“And for the case?”

“We can ask whether any documents discussed today belong in the ongoing investigation. The hearing doesn't substitute for that inquiry.”

He had known she would say it. Knowing did not protect him from disappointment.

Later Amina sent a brief message, not the minutes. She had gone home to eat, she wrote, and would review the official record tomorrow. Yanchuan replied that she should take the evening. He did not append another question.

At work Chen asked what had happened.

“A report was corrected,” Yanchuan said. “A new test was required. We still don't know where my brother went.”

Chen listened and asked whether Yanchuan needed the afternoon. He said he could finish one urgent calculation and take the rest.

On the train he watched sunlight come and go between buildings. A hearing held in daylight had produced no revelation about a hidden room or a secret enemy. It had required people to say exactly which records existed and what their signatures meant.

He imagined Yansheng at the counter in October, asking for the maintenance ticket and a record of its refusal. If the witness was right, his brother had wanted the same ordinary transparency the hearing had finally provided.

Yanchuan did not know whether Yansheng had lived long enough to see any of it. The thought stayed with him through the evening.

He put the hearing's official actions in one folder and the missing-person reference in another. The distinction was still necessary. It no longer felt like a refusal to acknowledge his brother.

It felt like a way to leave both questions open to the evidence that could answer them.
