The photograph of the room showed three desks, one cabinet, and a water stain on the ceiling.

Yanchuan had imagined it so often that the ordinary details offended him. There was no secret passage in the investigator's limited record, no locked interior door drawn on a plan. A document printer stood beside a cardboard box filled with spent toner cartridges. The cabinet had a label identifying retention periods.

“This is the room?” he asked Wanjiru.

“This is the document-receiving office whose records were examined. It isn't the entire compound.”

He understood. He had learned to hear the boundary in every useful sentence.

The authorized inspection had located the receiving log for a sealed packet delivered on October 30. The office code matched the courier's dispatch instruction. A staff member had signed for a packet of maintenance documents, but the log listed a packet number, not the names of every paper inside.

The packet itself was no longer on the receiving shelf. An archival transfer sheet indicated it had been moved into a contractor's document store in November. That store held work records for multiple customers. The investigators had requested the specific packet's inventory and current custodian.

“Could it be the missing restoration attachment?” Yanchuan asked.

“Possibly. We cannot say so until the contents are established.”

“Was my brother there?”

“The inspection has not confirmed that. The room is evidence of a document process, not of his presence.”

When the call ended, Yanchuan put the photograph beside the known visitor records. He wanted to annotate the office door with his brother's name. He wrote receiving office instead.

An joined him by video. Behind her, their son was trying to make a paper airplane that would circle back to the sofa. Each attempt landed on the floor. She looked at the photograph without speaking for a long time.

“I thought I'd recognize something,” she said.

“Like what?”

“I don't know. His handwriting on a box. The corner of his suitcase.”

Her admission relieved him. He had been ashamed of expecting a room to return a person.

“I hated that it looked ordinary,” he said.

“I hated that I was hoping it wouldn't,” An replied. She turned the photograph away so the boy could show her his airplane without seeing it on the screen.

Amina did not attend the inspection. Under the conditions she had set, she could answer technical questions about the equipment but would not become the family's guide through the case file. When she spoke to Yanchuan at their scheduled time, he asked about the archival code.

She checked the document index shared with authorized reviewers.

“It belongs to the contractor's record system,” she said. “The operator can request particular materials needed for the acceptance review. That's not the same as opening every customer's files.”

“So the investigator and reviewer ask separately.”

“Yes. And they may receive different parts for different reasons.”

He remembered how often he had wanted one folder containing everything. It would have been convenient for him and dangerous for people whose data belonged to other jobs.

Meanwhile Guo's project reached its first contract review. Chen showed the local client a proposed monitoring pilot for its peak-demand equipment. The pilot would measure demand, estimate how much could safely be shifted, and compare the results with the limits set by maintenance staff. It would not automatically alter machinery.

A commercial manager asked why the automation was disabled.

“Because we haven't shown that a suggestion is safe to execute,” Yanchuan said.

“That is what the pilot is for.”

“The pilot can test recommendations before it earns the authority to control anything.”

He could see the manager calculating whether the proposed benefit justified another month of human supervision. He could also see the maintenance technician beside the door, waiting to hear whether anyone would ask what happened on her night shift.

Chen asked her. She named two occasions when a planned shutdown had been delayed because a repair crew was late. The manager said those were exceptions. Yanchuan wrote them as scenarios that the pilot would need to handle.

On his train home, Wanjiru sent the next case update. The packet inventory had been found, but it identified documents by work-order codes rather than titles. One code matched the auxiliary cooling ticket. The record did not show whether the packet contained a signed restoration check or only correspondence requesting one.

The contents would be compared through the authorized channels.

Yanchuan stared at the code. For months he had searched for proof that something had been concealed. The most valuable item they had found was a reference whose interpretation still depended on what someone could actually produce.

He called Han Rui.

“Would you consider the ticket closed if the document room had filed a packet?”

Han laughed once.

“I'd consider a packet filed. Show me what the technician checked.”

“What if the record is gone?”

“Then the review says the evidence wasn't available. You don't invent a check because you wish someone had done one.”

Yanchuan thanked him and closed the call.

In Guangzhou, An's son finally made an airplane that curved toward the sofa. He sent Yanchuan a video with a triumphant shout. The camera moved so quickly that Yanchuan could barely see the paper before it landed.

He watched it twice. This time he was content to believe the boy had seen what he said he had seen.

At midnight Yanchuan printed the photograph of the office and placed it in the case folder. No belongings identified as Yansheng's had been reported from the inspection. No confirmed exit had been established. A packet had traveled through the room on a working day, and the documentation for that journey had survived.

He labeled the photograph with the room's actual function and the date the investigators had examined it.

The room had not proved what happened to his brother.

On the screen the office still looked like an office: three desks, one cabinet, a water stain on the ceiling. He closed the image before An's next call.
