Luo's apology arrived as an attachment to an expense reconciliation.

There was no fruit basket and no long statement about the company's concern. The document listed three movement claims that had been distributed during the first weeks of the search: that Yansheng had checked out of his hotel, that he had travelled to the airport, and that the service compound had recorded his departure.

The corrected version explained the source of each claim. A settled hotel account did not establish an observed checkout. A booked vehicle did not identify a passenger after its route changed. A returned visitor badge did not establish that its holder had left the compound.

Luo signed beneath the list and acknowledged that the earlier wording had been misleading.

An read it at her desk. The supplier accounts waited on her other screen.

“Did they send this to everyone who saw the first version?” she asked Yanchuan.

The covering email said the corrected account had been circulated to named recipients, including the employer's local representative and the case contact. There was a distribution record. An compared it with the earlier list and found two missing names.

She replied, thanking Luo for the corrections and asking for confirmation that the remaining recipients had been informed.

Yanchuan was surprised by the word thanking.

“He should have done it months ago,” he said.

“Yes.”

“You're not angry?”

“Of course I'm angry. I still want the wrong statement corrected.”

She did not confuse accepting a useful act with absolving the delay that preceded it.

Luo called later that afternoon. He said the company had found an old email in which Yansheng questioned whether the final report's sign-off procedure had been completed. The attachment did not show any request to abandon the assignment. It would be supplied to the inquiry through the established channel.

“Why wasn't this found earlier?” Yanchuan asked.

“Our first search was narrower,” Luo answered. “We searched travel messages and welfare correspondence. We didn't search all the technical mailboxes because the issue was treated separately.”

“And now?”

“Now the records are being indexed together for the people entitled to review them.”

The answer did not make the omission harmless. It was specific enough to reveal how it had happened and what had changed.

Wanjiru checked the permitted scope of disclosure. The email belonged in the technical review and could be considered by the case investigator if relevant. The family would receive the portion they were entitled to see, not an entire company mailbox.

Yanchuan found himself wanting everything, as he had in November. He also remembered the people whose work and private conversations lived in those accounts.

In the evening Chen asked why he had kept revising a client presentation after it was already accepted.

“We used a clean chart that hid a narrow operating limit,” Yanchuan said. “The client hasn't complained, but they should know.”

Chen reviewed the old and revised versions. There was no evidence the customer had relied on the missing detail for a harmful decision. Still, he agreed that a short correction should accompany the next scheduled review.

“Don't give them an apology that makes them do all our work,” he said. “Show the corrected limit, the affected conclusion and what remains unchanged.”

They sent it the next morning.

Yanchuan thought of Luo's list. An had asked for action: a corrected record, proper recipients, preserved originals and an accountable contact. It was possible to be sorry while still leaving the work to the person harmed. It was also possible to take useful responsibility without making a convincing performance of regret.

At home An's son had broken a wheel off his toy bus. He hid the wheel under a cushion, then told his mother it had always been loose.

“You knew it was broken,” An said.

He nodded, eyes on the floor.

“Did you try to fix it?”

“I thought Baba would.”

The room became quiet. An did not tell him his father would come home tomorrow, and she did not make the boy's small lie into a lesson about a missing man. She asked him to fetch the wheel and put it beside the bus so they would know which part needed repairing.

Later she told Yanchuan about it.

“He wanted to make the problem small enough that he didn't have to say it,” she said.

“Did you fix it?”

“Not yet. We put it in a box.”

Yanchuan looked at the picture of the box. Someone had written bus wheel on a slip of paper and tucked it under the lid.

The following Friday Luo's office confirmed delivery of the corrected statements to the two omitted recipients. It also processed the remaining reimbursable expenses that had been held without clear explanation. An checked the transfers against her account before acknowledging receipt.

The family did not sign anything saying the employer's responsibilities were complete.

When she told Jianming the reimbursement had finally arrived, he asked whether she trusted Luo now.

“I trust the amounts that have reached the account,” she said. “I'll judge the next thing when it comes.”

Their father was silent, then gave a small grunt of approval.

On the last page of the family chronology Yanchuan added a separate section for corrections obtained. It was longer than he expected. Their search had changed what institutions were willing to say about an absence, even as the absence itself remained.

He called Luo once, without An, to acknowledge the practical work.

“Thank you for sending the corrected records,” he said.

“I should have done it sooner,” Luo replied.

“Yes.”

Neither tried to improve the sentence.

After the call Yanchuan went back to work. A colleague had asked him to review a model that was being used outside its original test range. He marked the assumption, named the person who needed the result and sent a correction that did not require them to discover the problem again.

It was not heroic. It mattered.
