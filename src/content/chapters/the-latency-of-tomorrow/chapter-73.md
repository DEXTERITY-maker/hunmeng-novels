The receipt was folded inside a folder of household expenses because An had once mistaken it for a travel reimbursement.

It was not a new document. Yansheng had forwarded it to her months before his disappearance, asking whether a supplier's payment reference looked familiar. She had replied that the format was ordinary and filed the image with other work-related receipts.

Now, searching the old messages for a school-expense detail, she noticed the service account code printed at the bottom.

It matched the code that appeared in the contractor's annex records.

She called Yanchuan after putting their son to bed.

“Don't make the leap before you hear the whole thing,” she said.

The receipt concerned a small payment to a logistics and maintenance service provider. Its date was early October, weeks before the disputed acceptance test. The description referred to document handling, not transport of a passenger. It contained no name of a recipient at the compound.

“Could it be the same office?” Yanchuan asked.

“It's the same account family. That doesn't establish the same job.”

He enlarged the image. One field listed a work-order prefix that resembled the later maintenance ticket, but the remaining digits differed. He could not tell whether that reflected a recurring service or an unrelated transaction.

They preserved the original message and the image. An noted where she had stored it and asked Wanjiru whether the item could be relevant to the existing document-custody questions. She did not send it to the employer as an accusation or publish it with a theory.

Wanjiru replied that the receipt might help investigators identify the service provider's ordinary relationship to the assignment. It could also demonstrate that the account was used routinely, weakening an inference that the later envelope was unusual.

“Both possibilities matter,” she said during the call.

Yanchuan understood the second more slowly.

He had treated every shared code as a path toward an extraordinary event. A code that recurred before anything went wrong could mean the process was commonplace. The task was to discover what that ordinary process required and whether the October 30 movement departed from it.

An asked him to describe the receipt in the chronology without calling it an earlier warning.

He wrote: early-October service-payment reference; connection to later annex account under review; no verified relation to disappearance.

The line sat among other modest facts. None demanded a headline.

At work Arjun's project had reached a review of old maintenance receipts. A supplier's label for replacement tools had remained unchanged across several product revisions. The inventory team had assumed that matching descriptions meant interchangeable parts. Leela found two items that looked similar but required different specifications.

“Old receipts are useful,” she told Yanchuan on their scheduled call. “They're not substitute engineering drawings.”

He laughed. He felt as if everyone he knew had been assigned to correct his favorite kind of mistake.

The local team reconciled supplier records against current approved part numbers and physically checked the sample items through normal procedures. A planning model would use the verified mapping, not an optimistic assumption based on a shared label.

That evening Jianming called from Wuhan. He wanted to know whether the family's new receipt meant investigators had found the route Yansheng took.

“No,” Yanchuan said. “It's evidence that a service account was used before. We're asking what kind of work it covered.”

His father was silent for several moments.

“You sound like him,” he said at last.

“How?”

“He would come home with a paper and say it proved less than everyone wanted.”

Yanchuan looked at the archive window on his screen. He had spent years thinking of his brother as the older son who travelled too much and brought little gifts for his nephew. More of the man's working life had become visible since he went missing than Yanchuan had known when he was home.

The awareness brought pride and anger in equal measure.

Wanjiru later confirmed that the receipt had been forwarded through the appropriate channel. No immediate change in the case status followed. The family recorded that fact without calling the effort wasted.

An moved the original image into the indexed archive and left a copy in the household folder where it had first been found. She did not erase the accidental history of how they had kept it.

On Sunday their son helped sort a drawer of old school papers. He found a faded bus ticket from a trip with his father and asked whether they should throw it away.

An held it for a moment.

“Not today,” she said.

The ticket told them where a family had once travelled together. It was not a clue to a missing auditor's last movements. She placed it in a box of things their son might want to keep.

Yanchuan, listening on the call, did not ask to scan it into the case file. He had finally learned that a family's archive held more than evidence.
