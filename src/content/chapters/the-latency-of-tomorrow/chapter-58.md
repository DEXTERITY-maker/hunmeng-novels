The first thing Yanchuan got wrong at the workshop was the meaning of reserve.

He used the word to describe capacity a factory could keep available against a predictable peak or equipment failure. A local distribution engineer named Mutiso asked him what happened when the upstream supply was interrupted long enough for every planned shift to become irrelevant.

“We don't always hold reserve for the same hour,” Mutiso said. “Sometimes we're keeping a service alive until the road opens and fuel can arrive.”

Their conversation took place beside a wall map showing distribution lines, generators and customers whose loads varied across the week. The map was not a sales graphic. It had handwritten corrections where construction had changed access routes.

Yanchuan looked for the battery dispatch schedule.

“Which battery?” Mutiso asked.

He was embarrassed. Not every point on the map had storage equipment, and some that did were maintained under contracts with limits he did not yet understand.

In the morning session a technician described a cold-storage facility that could tolerate a short power transfer but not an unplanned maintenance gap of several hours. Another speaker explained that a clinic's refrigeration load was small in energy terms and enormous in consequence if it failed at the wrong time. A supplier described the difficulty of keeping spare parts nearby when equipment types changed faster than training and procurement systems.

Yanchuan had prepared slides about the price of availability. He took notes and began removing examples that assumed everyone could purchase the same kind of backup.

Amina watched him edit during lunch.

“Your talk is this afternoon,” she said.

“I know.”

“You could have done the polite version. Same slides, different city name.”

“I don't think they would have let me.”

“They might. Politeness is sometimes how we leave a room without learning anything.”

He asked whether the operator's corrected test had been scheduled. She said the preparatory work was continuing, and the technical group would share its own report when it was ready. The workshop was not a shortcut to restricted site records.

In his session he presented the domestic comparison without naming the overseas installation. He showed how two reports could agree on an efficiency total while answering different questions because their load boundaries differed. Then he invited the engineers to supply conditions under which the comparison would be misleading.

Mutiso immediately named an islanded supply whose generator could not be assumed to start within the usual transfer interval. Another engineer asked about a cooling load that varied with water availability. A maintenance supervisor pointed out that a theoretical spare was not a spare if the correct connector could not be obtained locally.

Yanchuan wrote every qualification beside the plot.

Afterward a young engineer named Asha asked whether his company's forecasting model could be trained on the local network.

“Possibly,” he said. “But first we'd have to know what measurements exist, what decisions it would support, and who would maintain it.”

“Then you aren't selling us a model?”

“Not today.”

She laughed. “Good. We already have people offering us models.”

They walked to the workshop yard, where a generator sat beneath a simple roof. Its maintenance board listed checks completed by named workers. Some rows were blank. The blanks did not mean the machine had never run; they meant the records needed clarification.

Asha said an optimization package offered by an outside firm had treated planned maintenance downtime as avoidable waste. The vendor had asked whether the equipment could be operated longer between inspections.

“Who decided the interval?” Yanchuan asked.

“The people who know the machine, and the contract that keeps it covered.”

He wondered whether the vendor had ever seen the board.

That evening he called An. The sound of traffic came through his hotel window. He described the map, the local engineers and the assumptions he had removed from his presentation.

“Did you learn anything about Yansheng?” she asked.

“Nothing new today.”

“And was the workshop useful?”

“Yes.”

“Then it's all right for the day to have been useful without being a lead.”

He had needed someone else to say it.

The next morning Amina introduced him to a maintenance team that was preparing the corrected acceptance process. He attended only the discussion open to workshop participants. The technicians described tests that would verify restoration of the auxiliary cooling arrangement and establish a load profile representative of the service actually promised.

No one provided the missing-person case file. No one asked him to inspect a cabinet or unlock a network terminal.

At lunch Mutiso asked why Yanchuan had come back to Kenya. He told the truth in two parts: he had been invited for the engineering exchange, and his brother remained missing here.

Mutiso listened and said he was sorry. Then he asked whether the family had local legal help. When Yanchuan said yes, Mutiso did not offer an informal investigation of his own.

Instead he gave Yanchuan a copy of the workshop's publicly shareable maintenance framework and told him to check whether any recommendations he made from Shenzhen would be useful under these conditions.

On the flight back later that week, Yanchuan opened his original pilot model. It contained choices about power supply, maintenance access and operator authority that he had treated as ordinary. They were ordinary in the places where he had measured them. They were not laws of nature.

He added a requirement to the next project's scope: the operating network map must be prepared or confirmed by local engineers before the financial model assigns a value to reserve capacity. Mutiso read the line and asked for one more condition: the map must be updated when the rainy season changes access.
