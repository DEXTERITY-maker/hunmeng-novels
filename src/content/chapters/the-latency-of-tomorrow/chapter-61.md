The cheaper loan had the more expensive promise.

Guo placed two financing proposals on the table. One offered a lower interest rate for expanding the industrial forecasting service. In return, the provider would have to maintain a minimum rate of customer savings, calculated quarterly. The second cost more in interest but made payments depend on delivered engineering work, with operating outcomes evaluated separately.

“Which would you choose?” he asked Yanchuan.

“I'm not choosing financing for you.”

“Then tell me what the cheaper one requires the engineers to say.”

The answer occupied several pages. The savings measure excluded periods classified as unavailable, and the lender's draft allowed the provider to propose such classifications. A project under pressure to hit a quarterly target could improve its numbers by deciding that difficult operating periods were outside the comparison.

“You wouldn't do that,” Guo said.

“That isn't a control,” Yanchuan replied.

They asked a lawyer and the factory operator to review the definition. The lender agreed to a tighter procedure for excluding events, but wanted the same target. Gao read the final version and pointed to a heat-related stop condition.

“If my team preserves the reserve, the project might miss the target. Who pays?”

Guo had assumed the provider would absorb the financial penalty. The provider's margins were thin enough that several such quarters could threaten the service itself. The risk would not vanish. It would travel from the lender to the company and eventually to the people whose wages depended on it.

They ran the scenarios. The cheaper capital was cheaper only when the system performed inside a narrow range and nobody needed to exercise the human stop often. Outside that range, the penalties were severe.

Chen told Guo the comparison had made the decision easy.

“Not easy,” Guo said. “Visible.”

He chose to negotiate the second offer, seeking smaller payments and a longer term without surrendering the ability to report unfavorable outcomes. The investor on the other side wanted a clearer path to commercial growth. Guo promised to return with additional customers only after the pilot results were documented honestly.

At dinner Ning asked whether they were now in the business of telling financiers how to read footnotes.

“We're in the business of making engineering claims,” Yanchuan said. “They keep finding ways to sell them.”

“And we keep finding people who might be asked to live inside those claims,” she answered.

A year had passed since the first archive arrived from Yansheng. The family still had no definitive answer. A technical finding had been issued, testimony assessed and records preserved. None had established a final account of his whereabouts. An had stopped measuring her days by how long the investigation had gone without a new message. She measured what tasks were due, what questions had received answers and what her son needed the next morning.

When Yanchuan told her about the financing decision, she asked whether the new agreement would give his team enough time to maintain the systems they built.

“That's part of the negotiation.”

“It should be a line in the budget, not a promise you'll work late.”

He thought about that after the call. Their first paid pilot had depended on overtime nobody included in the selling price. The next contract would need a realistic support schedule and people able to leave work without abandoning a customer.

Guo's firm eventually secured the revised financing. It cost more on paper than the first proposal. The agreement retained a documented safety override, independent reporting of performance limits and a schedule of maintenance obligations whose costs could not simply be removed from the accounts.

At the signing Guo asked Yanchuan whether the cautious arrangement would slow expansion.

“Probably.”

“Will it make us better?”

“Not automatically. It gives us a chance to find out without being rewarded for hiding the answer.”

Guo smiled at that, as if he had expected precisely such an unpromising endorsement.

The following week a prospective partner from India called. His factory had equipment faults that interrupted production, and he wanted to discuss whether a small forecasting system could help. He was not asking for power-market trading or a polished capacity brochure. He wanted fewer unexpected stoppages and records his maintenance staff could trust.

Guo forwarded the request to Chen with a note: Perhaps the capital is finally buying the work we know how to do.

Yanchuan opened a folder for the Indian factory's inquiry. Chen asked for a list of people who would maintain the system after the demonstration, before authorizing a price. Guo forwarded the request without deleting that line. The family's case file stayed where it had been, open to its own next response.
