The alarm sounded while the pilot was recommending a cheaper cooling schedule.

Gao was crossing the factory floor when the control screen flagged a rise in temperature on the secondary equipment loop. The system had not issued a switching command. It displayed a suggestion to delay a nonessential cooling load, based on a forecast of falling demand in the next half hour.

Gao rejected the suggestion and followed the plant's existing procedure. A technician checked the readings against a local instrument. The two disagreed slightly, but both showed the temperature moving toward the limit. The crew opened the approved backup path and reduced demand until the loop stabilized.

Nobody was injured and the temperature stabilized, but Gao remained beside the local gauge after the alarms cleared. He asked the technician to read the number back without looking at the pilot display. Only then did he initial the shift log. The event occupied fourteen minutes.

Yanchuan saw the duration afterward and felt his stomach contract. He had promised himself not to turn every fourteen-minute period into a reflection of his brother. Numbers did not become evidence by resembling one another.

Ning asked the technician to explain the sequence. A valve had responded slowly after routine maintenance. It was not a failure of the forecasting model. It was not proof that the model would have caused an incident if an operator had accepted its suggestion. The two events had overlapped, and that overlap mattered because the recommended reduction might have reduced the available margin.

The pilot recorded an operational stop condition and withheld further recommendations affecting the loop until the maintenance team cleared it.

Guo arrived for the review that afternoon.

“Did the system catch the problem?” he asked.

“The plant alarms did,” Gao said. “The pilot recommended something else. We declined it.”

“Would it have known not to recommend that if the valve information had been in the model?”

“Possibly,” Ning said. “We don't know until we test the revised input and failure response.”

The commercial manager said it looked bad for a project nearing its first formal expansion decision. Yanchuan agreed that it looked bad in a presentation. He did not agree that hiding it would improve the system.

They reconstructed the state of the data at the time. The maintenance log had marked the valve available, and no fault had been recorded when the recommendation was generated. Operators nevertheless had a direct view of the local temperature and authority to stop. The system had been designed to depend on that decision.

“This is why the stop matters,” Yanchuan said.

Guo examined the log. “Then the human intervention is part of the product?”

“The capability to intervene is part of the service,” Ning replied. “The particular decision belonged to Gao's team.”

They kept the distinction in the report. The pilot had generated suggestions, measured their outcomes and exposed failure conditions. It had not earned the authority to run unattended.

The next day Chen gathered the team to decide whether to extend the pilot. They reviewed the savings, the denied recommendations, the sensor faults, the maintenance handovers and the periods when forecasts arrived too late to be useful. No one person could describe the entire operation. Gao spoke for the workers who would live with the consequences. Ning spoke for the control systems. Yanchuan spoke for the limits of the forecast. Guo spoke for the money that had to make the arrangement sustainable.

The decision was smaller than the original ambition. The trial would continue with advisory recommendations only. Any move toward automatic control would require a new safety assessment, agreed operating procedures, independent testing and explicit responsibility for stopping the system.

“Not a vote of confidence?” the commercial manager asked.

“Confidence in what we've actually tested,” Chen said.

They signed a short record of the decision. It included the unresolved temperature event, not merely the savings achieved.

After the meeting Guo asked Yanchuan whether he was disappointed.

“I wanted it to work.”

“It did some useful work.”

“Not everything we hoped.”

“Then our next investment decision should reflect that.”

He seemed almost cheerful. Yanchuan did not know whether Guo was pleased by the discipline of the process or by the prospect of acquiring a promising service before competitors understood its value. Both could be true.

At the railway station An called. Their son was asleep after a school trip. She had just reviewed the family's latest expense reimbursements and found that the employer had finally paid several items without demanding any acknowledgment that the search was over.

“That's good,” Yanchuan said.

“It's ordinary money they owed us.”

“Yes.”

“I can still be glad it came.”

He agreed. They spoke about the new pilot decision and the fourteen-minute operational stop. He told her the duration without making it a symbol.

“What did the operator do?” she asked.

“He rejected a recommendation and followed the safety procedure.”

“Then the person responsible was able to decide.”

It was a simple description of the day's most important result.

That evening Jianming asked whether the factory's system would eventually be smarter than its engineers.

“Maybe about some things,” Yanchuan said. “It already sees patterns we can't follow in real time.”

“Then why does it need them?”

“Because it doesn't know everything that matters. And because someone still has to be responsible for what it does.”

His father said that sounded like the advice he used to give both sons when they were young and repairing radios.

“Test the part,” Jianming said. “Don't forget the circuit.”

After the call Yanchuan read the first message Yansheng had sent with the archive. His brother had not asked for a rescue. He had asked whether two summaries could both be right. The question had led to a technical correction, an unfinished official inquiry and work Yanchuan could now perform differently.

He closed the message without blaming himself for not opening it sooner. The regret remained. It no longer had to control every choice.

The factory's first-phase report lay on his desk. It contained a human refusal, a stopped recommendation and a smaller but defensible account of the benefit. The next phase would require different contracts, more people and harder questions about who bore the cost.

He signed the engineering section and sent it to Chen.

The manual decision was not a failure to automate.

It was the condition under which the rest of the work could begin.
