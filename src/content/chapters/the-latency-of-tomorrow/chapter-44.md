Yansheng's name appeared in a public notice on a Monday morning without anyone asking his family first.

The notice concerned the industrial acceptance review. It stated that a previous performance claim had not been substantiated by the records examined and that a revised acceptance process would be required. In a list of contributors to the original review it named Lin Yansheng as an auditor who had raised questions about the test boundary.

There was no accusation against him. There was also no mention that he was missing.

An forwarded the notice to Wanjiru, then called Yanchuan from the supplier office.

“Can they put his name there?”

“That depends on the publication rules,” he said, realizing as he spoke that he did not know them.

“We should ask before we tell anyone to remove it.”

She had already drafted the request. It asked for the authority to publish a contributor's identity, the source of the description, and whether the family could correct factual errors without being asked to endorse the notice as a whole.

Yanchuan searched his brother's archive. The original message had asked whether two summaries could both be right. The public notice described a formal objection at the review meeting. That might have happened. He had not seen an original minute that established Yansheng's words.

He wanted to be proud that his brother's question was being acknowledged. He was afraid that a sentence in a notice could become the entire public version of a man.

Luo called that evening. He said the employer had learned of the notice at the same time as the family. Yanchuan asked whether the firm had supplied Yansheng's name. Luo could not answer immediately and promised to check.

“You told us he might have changed his plans,” An said when she joined the call. “Will you now tell your clients he raised a concern?”

“We will use the corrected account,” Luo said.

“Please send the wording before calling it corrected.”

He did not object. Months of imprecise statements had made the family exacting, and Luo had begun to understand that an acknowledgment was not a favor when it corrected the firm's own records.

The next day Wanjiru obtained a clarification. The notice was issued by the operator under its reporting process. The contributor list came from the earlier attendance sheet and technical correspondence. The wording about Yansheng raising questions was supported by a request for records but did not purport to quote a particular meeting.

The name could remain. The family could submit a request for factual correction if it identified an error. The question of whether to mention the disappearance in the technical notice was separate and needed careful consideration.

An read the explanation twice. She did not insist that the notice become a missing-person appeal. A technical correction served people whose work and equipment depended on the record. They already had a public appeal for information about her husband.

But she asked for one thing: the notice must not imply that Yansheng had approved the final package. The operator agreed to state plainly that an attendance acknowledgment was not an acceptance signature.

It took another revision and six emails. The final sentence was short.

Yanchuan showed his father.

“So he didn't sign the approval,” Jianming said.

“The available record doesn't show that he authorized the final claim.”

“You always make the simple thing difficult.”

“It was difficult before I said it.”

His father looked at the printed notice he had brought to Wuhan. He traced his son's English name with one finger.

“Will his boy read this when he's older?”

“He might.”

“Then keep the original as well.”

Yanchuan promised to preserve both versions with their dates.

At home An's son had been asked to write a paragraph about someone whose work mattered. He wanted to write about his father. An asked what he remembered Yansheng doing.

“He fixed the tap,” the boy said.

“He meant to fix it,” An corrected gently. “We had someone else do it later.”

“He taught me the wide bridge.”

“Yes.”

“He was at work a lot.”

“Yes.”

The boy considered the answers and asked whether he had to say what job his father did.

“Only if you want to,” An said. “You can write what you know.”

The finished paragraph described a man who sometimes traveled with a suitcase and asked whether a paper bridge would hold a toy bus. It mentioned that he checked things carefully. There was no industrial report, no fourteen-minute gap, no theory of why he did not come home.

An sent Yanchuan a photograph of the page. He sat with it longer than he had sat with the public notice.

At the bottom his nephew had drawn the bridge in blue pencil. The description of his father took up only four lines. Yanchuan nearly suggested adding the man's engineering work, then remembered how carefully An had let the boy choose his own memories.

Mei called later and asked whether he wanted to add a short line to the family's appeal acknowledging the technical finding.

“Would it help locate him?” he asked.

“That's the question you should ask before publishing it.”

He considered the routes by which a person might learn about the case. The notice could prompt a former colleague to remember a conversation. It could also encourage people to invent a story linking the report to the disappearance because the two facts had been printed near one another.

They asked Wanjiru whether a factual update through the established appeal channel would be appropriate. She suggested a limited note identifying the last confirmed contact and saying that work-related questions were under separate review, without accusing anyone of a crime.

An approved it. They did not attach the child's paragraph.

On the final page of the family chronology, Yanchuan added two references: the operator's amended notice and the school's writing assignment. He did not connect them with an arrow.

One described work that had to be made publicly accurate. The other described a father whom the work could never fully explain.

That evening An called while washing dishes. The bathroom tap was no longer dripping. The blue bowl had been moved into a cupboard.

She told Yanchuan she had kept it.

He wanted to ask why she had kept the bowl if she no longer wanted it on the table. He recognized that the question would ask her to explain a grief he had no right to inspect.
