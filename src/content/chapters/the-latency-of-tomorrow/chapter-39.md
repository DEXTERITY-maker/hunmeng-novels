The city lost a substation shortly before dawn, and Yanchuan learned of it because the bakery across from his apartment had no lights.

He was leaving early for a factory review. A hand-written sign on the bakery door said the ovens would start when the power returned. Two women waited beneath the awning anyway, checking their phones. The outage had not reached his building, whose lobby lights and lifts were still operating.

At the office Chen had a message from the utility liaison. A feeder fault had interrupted several industrial customers. Their pilot factory was on another circuit and had suffered no loss of supply. Nevertheless, the company's management wanted to know whether its new forecasting tool had predicted the interruption.

“It forecasts our demand,” Ning said. “It doesn't inspect the utility's switches.”

The manager asked if they could add that.

“We could add information supplied by the utility,” Yanchuan replied. “We couldn't promise to predict a cable failure from our production schedule.”

He had begun to recognize the moment a useful capability invited someone to imagine another, larger capability behind it.

The factory had continued operating. Most of the day-shift workers arrived. One supervisor was late because traffic signals near the outage had failed and the buses were rerouted. A cleaning crew could not collect supplies from a locked warehouse whose electronic door remained unpowered. Gao asked the pilot team to record those effects under disruptions rather than energy savings.

“They don't show up on your meter,” he said. “They still change what we can do.”

At noon the utility published a limited notice: an equipment fault, affected districts, restoration crews at work, no reliable estimate yet for every customer. Online comments demanded a more confident timetable. Yanchuan caught himself thinking about which forecasting model might satisfy them, then noticed that the technicians still had to inspect the actual failed equipment.

He went to the bakery after work. The ovens were running, but the owner had discarded a batch of dough she could not safely hold at the required temperature. The till showed the day's sales. It did not show what had gone into the bins.

“Do you have insurance for interruptions?” Yanchuan asked.

“Do you have insurance for every question you ask?” she replied, then smiled enough to show that the rebuke was not personal.

He bought bread. On the receipt a small line identified a discount for the delayed opening. It was less than the cost of the ingredients wasted that morning.

That evening Ning sent the factory's revised record. Under the heading service availability, the production manager had entered that all essential machines were powered throughout the utility event. Under staffing, the delayed supervisor had been marked absent for twenty-three minutes. Under materials, the cleaning crew's missed collection remained an unresolved task. The same event could be described as no electrical interruption and a day of small operational losses.

Yanchuan proposed including those categories in the pilot report as context rather than attributing them to their model. Gao approved the wording. A financial manager complained that it complicated the success story.

“It is a success that the plant kept running,” Yanchuan said. “It is also useful to know what almost stopped it.”

Later his father called from Wuhan. The building lift had been repaired. Jianming had watched the mechanic replace a worn contactor and then insisted on taking a short ride before paying the maintenance share.

“Did he mind?” Yanchuan asked.

“He said I was the first resident who wanted to test it instead of thanking him.”

“Did you?”

“I thanked him after it worked.”

They spoke about the repair until Jianming asked for news of Yansheng. There was no new official finding. The answer was still no.

For once his father did not tell him to ask someone another question. He said the old street outside the building was being repaved again. People kept walking around the barriers to reach the shops. The work was inconvenient, he said, but he was glad somebody was replacing the broken drains before the next heavy rain.

Yanchuan thought of the factory's uninterrupted machines, the bakery's spoiled dough, the invisible detours by which a city continued functioning.

He wrote to An, asking whether she and the boy had lost power. They had not. She sent him a photograph of a school notice warning parents about changes to bus arrival times. He offered to help the teachers reorganize the routes with a spreadsheet. She told him not to volunteer a system before the school had asked for one.

He set the phone aside. Then he checked the factory report again and restored a line the finance manager had removed: continuity of electrical supply does not demonstrate continuity of every dependent service.

It was an awkward sentence. He shortened it: The power stayed on. Some work still stopped.

The next morning the bakery opened on time. The sign had been removed. Yanchuan bought a warm bun and stood at the counter long enough to see the owner checking the oven temperature with a separate thermometer.

He did not ask her to explain the reading.

In the city beyond the glass, delivery carts crossed repaired intersections. Maintenance vans moved through traffic. A school bus waited while a child climbed aboard. Most of the people keeping those systems useful would never appear on the dashboards that praised them.

He finished breakfast and went to work.
