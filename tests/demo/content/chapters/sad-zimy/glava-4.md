The first flower opened on Monday, while the city was still asleep. It was tiny and white, and at first Nika mistook it for a scrap of paper.

By noon, neighbors had gathered beside the greenhouse. No one asked whether winter was over. They brought chairs, flasks, and a board on which they wrote a reading schedule.

The gardener handed Nika his straw hat. She asked where he was going. He pointed to the book about birds: a path had appeared on the last page.

“There are gardens where no one has ever read a single story,” he said. “I shall visit them. You can take care of this one now.”

Nika wanted to protest, but just then the clock in the square struck one. For the first time in months, its chimes sounded less like a reminder of time and more like an invitation.

She opened the first book. People drew their chairs closer. Snow still lay outside, but inside, spring had already begun.
